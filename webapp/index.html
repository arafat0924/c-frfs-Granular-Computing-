Results

Macro-F1, mean over 5 folds; higher is better and every class counts equally. The small number is the spread between folds; gaps smaller than it are ties.

Method

FRFS. Fuzzy-rough feature selection adds columns one at a time, always the one that makes the most records clearly belong to their class. Its cost grows with the square of the row count, so it runs on 300-row samples.

The consensus. C-FRFS runs FRFS 30 times, each on a fresh 300-row sample, and keeps the columns that at least half the runs picked. The kept columns are original, readable ones, unlike PCA.

The test. 5-fold cross-validation on 20,000 rows, k = 12 columns for every method, scored with kNN and a decision tree. Compared with all columns, one FRFS run on 300 or 1,000 rows, mutual information, chi-squared and PCA.

Data. NSL-KDD (41 columns, 5 classes) and CIC-IoT2023 (39 columns, benign plus 7 attack families).

Limits

  • Not more accurate than one FRFS run. The vote makes the pick more repeatable, but the scores sit inside the fold-to-fold spread.
  • All columns win on CIC-IoT2023. Every 12-column set, C-FRFS included, scores lower than using all 39.
  • Column order matters. On 300 rows FRFS reaches its best score after about 3 columns; later picks are ties settled by column order. A re-run that breaks ties at random keeps only about 3 columns.
  • NSL-KDD is not IoT traffic. The IoT claims rest on CIC-IoT2023 only, a capped third-party copy.
  • One seed, one sample, k = 12 not tuned. NSL-KDD's rarest class has 8 rows.
